# Glossary

**Algorithmic judgment** — using explicit formulas or scoring rules instead of holistic human judgment (Ch 21).
**Anchoring effect** — estimates are pulled toward an initial number, even when arbitrary (Ch 11).
**Availability cascade** — public concern amplifies when vivid examples become easy to recall and repeat (Ch 13).
**Availability heuristic** — judging frequency or importance by ease of recall (Ch 12).
**Base rate** — the prior distribution or reference-class frequency that should anchor prediction (Ch 14, Ch 16, Ch 18).
**Cognitive ease** — the feeling of fluency that makes statements feel familiar, true, and pleasant (Ch 5).
**Conjunction fallacy** — judging a combined event as more likely than one of its components (Ch 15).
**Decision weight** — psychological weight assigned to probability, often unlike objective probability (Ch 29).
**Endowment effect** — ownership increases valuation and makes giving up feel like a loss (Ch 27).
**Experiencing self** — the self that lives moment-by-moment experience (Ch 35, Ch 37).
**Expert intuition** — reliable only in regular environments with repeated feedback (Ch 22).
**Focusing illusion** — nothing is as important as it seems while attention is focused on it (Ch 38).
**Framing effect** — equivalent descriptions cause different choices by triggering different emotions or reference points (Ch 34).
**Halo effect** — early impressions spill over into unrelated evaluations (Ch 7).
**Heuristic substitution** — answering an easier question instead of the target question (Ch 9).
**Illusion of validity** — confidence created by a coherent story despite weak predictive evidence (Ch 20).
**Inside view** — forecast built from the specific plan and its story (Ch 23).
**Law of small numbers** — mistaken belief that small samples closely resemble the population (Ch 10).
**Loss aversion** — losses loom larger than equivalent gains (Ch 26).
**Mental accounting** — treating money or outcomes as separate psychological accounts (Ch 32).
**Outside view** — forecast from reference-class outcomes before case-specific adjustment (Ch 23).
**Peak-end rule** — memories of experiences overweight the peak and ending (Ch 35, Ch 36).
**Planning fallacy** — underestimating time, cost, and obstacles by relying on the inside view (Ch 23).
**Premortem** — imagining a future failure and listing causes before committing (Ch 24).
**Prospect theory** — model of choice under risk based on reference points, loss aversion, and decision weights (Ch 26, Ch 29).
**Regression to the mean** — extreme outcomes are likely to be followed by less extreme outcomes (Ch 17).
**Remembering self** — the self that stores and evaluates experience as a story (Ch 35, Ch 36).
**Representativeness heuristic** — judging probability by similarity to a stereotype or prototype (Ch 14).
**Risk policy** — broad rule for accepting repeated favorable risks despite local losses (Ch 31).
**System 1** — fast, automatic, associative, effortless cognition (Ch 1-9).
**System 2** — slow, controlled, effortful cognition and self-monitoring (Ch 1-3).
**WYSIATI** — what you see is all there is; coherent stories are built from available evidence (Ch 7).
