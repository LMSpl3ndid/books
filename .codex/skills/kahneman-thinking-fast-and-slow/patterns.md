# Patterns

## Reference-Class Forecasting
**When to use**: project timelines, budgets, hiring, forecasts, strategy.
**How**: define the reference class; collect actual outcomes; place the current case in that distribution; adjust only for clearly diagnostic differences.
**Trade-offs**: less flattering than the inside view, but more calibrated.

## Four-Step Intuitive Prediction Correction
**When to use**: predicting performance from noisy impressions.
**How**: start with base rate; make an intuitive rank; estimate evidence validity; move prediction only partially away from the mean.
**Trade-offs**: feels conservative, but prevents extreme forecasts from weak evidence.

## Premortem
**When to use**: plans with optimism, group pressure, or high switching costs.
**How**: assume the project failed badly; independently list causes; aggregate; redesign plan around preventable causes.
**Trade-offs**: may dampen morale if handled poorly, but reveals hidden risks.

## Independent-Judgment Before Discussion
**When to use**: hiring, investment committees, product reviews, risk scoring.
**How**: have each person record evidence and rating before group discussion; aggregate before debate.
**Trade-offs**: slower upfront, less vulnerable to halo effects and anchoring.

## Broad Framing For Repeated Risks
**When to use**: portfolios, experiments, bets with positive expected value.
**How**: evaluate the policy over many repetitions, not the emotional impact of one loss.
**Trade-offs**: requires discipline and enough independence between bets.

## Anchor Reset
**When to use**: negotiation, estimation, roadmapping.
**How**: identify the anchor; state why it is irrelevant or weak; generate an independent estimate from base data; negotiate from that range.
**Trade-offs**: cannot erase the anchor completely, but reduces adjustment insufficiency.

## Mental-Account Closure
**When to use**: sunk costs, losing investments, unused tickets, delayed projects.
**How**: ignore past unrecoverable costs; compare future options from today forward; document the opportunity cost of continuing.
**Trade-offs**: emotionally hard because it realizes a loss.

## Frame Audit
**When to use**: policy wording, medical choices, product defaults, risk communication.
**How**: express the same option as gains, losses, frequencies, percentages, and defaults; check whether preference changes.
**Trade-offs**: reveals instability; may require choosing a transparent frame deliberately.
